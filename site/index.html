How it plays

What you do each month

One month is one turn. Take as long as you like, or let the clock run.

01

Design

Start from a ready-made server or build your own from CPUs, memory, GPUs, network cards and power supplies, then stack servers and switches in a rack.

Power, weight and cooling are checked as you go, and the game lists what will not work before you pay for it.

02

Order

Parts take months to arrive and vendors ration the popular ones. Buy loose parts, or pay more for a rack that turns up assembled.

About 700 real parts: A100 systems from 2020 through to Vera Rubin, AMD Instinct, Gaudi, Cerebras, Tenstorrent, and RTX cards if you want to build cheap. Prices move with demand.

03

Build

The hall needs enough power and cooling for what you put in it. Air cooling runs out at about 22 kW a rack; after that it is rear-door, liquid or immersion.

Go over the limit and racks shut down. Transformers, UPS, generators and chillers are all yours to buy and size.

04

Lease

Training customers rent a whole cluster by the GPU-hour. Inference customers pay per million tokens. Both leave if you miss the uptime you promised.

Each offer says whether your hardware can serve it and what you would still have to buy. Easy Path does the whole job for you as parts arrive, and idle capacity can go on the spot market.

05

Cluster

Group racks into one machine and pick its network, or let the game design one.

You then see what it can do: tokens per second, training utilisation, time to first token. Dense and mixture-of-experts models load the network differently, and the numbers show it.

06

Survive

Parts fail, and hardware loses value every year, faster when a new GPU generation ships. Investors review you every January.

Three months in a row with no cash ends the game. There are six endings, each with a scored report.

Cosmetics

How it is built

Godot 4 front-end

Godot draws the hall and the interface and handles the controller. It talks to the rules engine over a JSON line protocol: a Node process on desktop, in-process on Android.

Deterministic engine

All the rules (power, cooling, networking, markets) are TypeScript. The same seed always gives the same game, and tests compare whole recorded campaigns against it.

Scripted players

Six scripted play styles run full 97-month campaigns in the test suite and check the game state for nonsense every month.

An RL environment

The engine has a step/reset interface and a Python wrapper, if you want to train an agent on it.
